# Three formalisms of work, one structure

The work vocabulary in [library/theories/humanities/theories/sociology/theories/work/](../../../) carries three distinct mathematical formalisms across its texts. Each is well-developed, internally consistent, and answers a different question about the same underlying content. Predicated-work treats them as *parallel presentations* of one structure — different coordinate charts on the same manifold of work.

## The three charts

**1. Bilattice-substrate (predicated-work).** This program. Operations are settlings of a [predication-settling](../../../../../../../../../library/theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/objects/predication-settling.md) over a work-themed predicate category. Tasks, plans, workflows live as predication-cell sets with goal valuations; dispatch is residuation; completion is settling-quiescence; the four-regime classification typologizes work by closure-ordinal structure. The chart's strength: it inherits the math program's settling dynamics, regime classification, cohomology, and moduli — work is a structure inside a categorical apparatus that already has its own proved theorems.

**2. Heyting-presheaf with nuclear closures.** The work texts [formal-structure-of-office](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../science-services-section/services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/documents/notes/2026-04-03-note-on-formal-structure-of-office.md) and [formal-structure-of-delegation](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../science-services-section/services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/content/documents/notes/2026-04-03-note-on-formal-structure-of-delegation.md) develop offices and delegations as Heyting algebras $H_t$ at each history $t$, with two nuclear closures $\sigma_t$ (recognition) and $\Delta_t$ (induction) whose joint fixed points $H^*_t = \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_t) \cap \mathrm{Fix}(\Delta_t)$ name doubly-stable authority. Restriction maps in a presheaf $H : T^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{HA}$ encode delegation; the four shadow-class table (Proposition 2 in the delegation text) characterizes derived-grant types. The chart's strength: the nuclear-doctrine apparatus is mature in topos theory, the Heyting-algebra structure carries directly to derived-equivalent presentations, and the Roman legal correspondence (*munus / honor / dignitas*) lands at clean structural conditions.

**3. Free-algebra ground-terms with substitution closure.** The text [algebraic-structure-of-work](../../../texts/algebraic-structure-of-work.md) develops operations, work, and work units as universal-algebra term-structures: signatures $\Sigma$ provide operation symbols with arities; work is term application $\omega(a_1, \ldots, a_n)$ possibly with free variables; work units are ground terms; settlement closes free variables via substitution. The four work-types (process, procedure, derivation, inquiry) correspond to the four positions in the inquiry → process settlement path. Plan dependencies impose equations on the free term algebra, generating a quotient $T(\Sigma, X) / E$. The chart's strength: it captures the *combinatorial* structure of work composition cleanly, with the work-type classification falling out of the substitution closure.

## What each chart sees clearly that the others see less clearly

- **Bilattice-substrate** sees temporal evolution and epistemic state best. Settling-monotonicity, four-regime classification, and the bilattice's four-valued evidence structure are first-class. Static-structure questions (offices, authorities, terms) require additional scaffolding (jural-relation entities, sub-bases for jurisdiction, etc.).

- **Heyting-presheaf** sees authority structure best. The fixed-point conditions for incumbency, the restriction-map structure of delegation, the geometric morphisms for jurisdiction are immediate. Temporal dynamics and combinatorial composition require additional encoding.

- **Free-algebra** sees combinatorial composition best. Operation/work/work-unit typing, plan dependencies as equations, settlement-as-substitution are immediate. Temporal dynamics and authority structure are not native.

## Translation between charts

The three charts are equivalent presentations in the same sense that linear algebra over $\mathbb{R}^n$ has multiple coordinate systems: the same content under different parametrizations. Specific translations:

- **Heyting → Bilattice.** Per [office-formalism-equivalence theorem](../theories/predicated-governance/theorems/office-formalism-equivalence.md), the Heyting algebra fiber $H_t$ corresponds to the fiber-positive-coordinate $A^+_p$ of a predication fiber algebra; the two nuclear closures correspond to two closure rules on two separate predicates (institution / induction); the joint fixed-point set corresponds to joint $\mathbf{t}$-settlement of both predicate cells; restriction maps correspond to settling-morphisms.
- **Free-algebra → Bilattice.** A ground term $\omega(c_1, \ldots, c_n)$ corresponds to a [task-cell-system](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../science-services-section/services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/predicated-work-services-section/content/glossary/terms/task-cell-system.md) whose cells encode the operation symbol and its bound parameters. Substitution of free variables corresponds to closure-rule firings that settle the variable cells. Plan dependencies as equations $E$ correspond to closure-rule constraints in the [decompose-task morphism](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../science-services-section/services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/predicated-work-services-section/content/glossary/terms/decompose-task.md). The four work-types correspond to four classes of closure-rule subsystems (cycle-independent, finite-history, etc., per [regime-of-recurrent-practice](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../science-services-section/services/social-science-services-section/services/sociology-science-services-section/services/work-science-services-section/services/predicated-work-services-section/content/glossary/concepts/regime-of-recurrent-practice.md)).
- **Heyting ↔ Free-algebra.** Less directly developed; the Heyting algebra of plan-dependency equations is a quotient algebra in the universal-algebra sense (the term algebra modulo $E$), and the presheaf structure $H : T^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{HA}$ adds temporal indexing to that quotient. Whether this is a clean translation requires more work.

## Why three charts and not one

The charts emerged from different traditions. The Heyting-presheaf chart is grounded in topos theory and Roman/medieval legal doctrine (Kantorowicz, Plowden, Gierke). The free-algebra chart is grounded in universal algebra (Lambek-categorical-grammar lineage, Lawvere theories) and AI planning (STRIPS, partial-order plans, PDDL). The bilattice-substrate chart is grounded in many-valued logic (Belnap, Fitting), bilattices (Arieli-Avron, Jung-Rivieccio), and category theory of fibrations (Lawvere hyperdoctrines, Maruyama).

Each tradition has its own mature theorems. The work vocabulary chose to import all three rather than reduce to one. Predicated-work's role is not to subsume the other two but to make the *bilattice-substrate version* available with full math-program inheritance, while keeping the other charts referenceable as alternative presentations.

## What this means operationally

For a specific operational question, pick the chart that sees it clearly:

- *Is this incumbency stable?* → Heyting-presheaf (joint-fixed-point check).
- *Will this plan settle?* → Free-algebra (substitution closure under the dependency equations).
- *What kind of work-pattern is this practice?* → Bilattice-substrate (regime placement).
- *What's the cohomological invariant of this function?* → Bilattice-substrate (aperiodic-regime classification by $H^1$-class).
- *Does this delegation chain have proper bounds?* → Heyting-presheaf (presheaf functoriality).
- *Can these workflows compose?* → Free-algebra (type matching at boundaries).

The translation theorems (where they exist) let operationally-different questions feed back into one another. Where translations are not yet worked out, the charts are independent — using one's machinery doesn't automatically make the other's machinery available on the same content.

## Position in the program

This narrative anchors the *parallel-formalisms* commitment of predicated-work. Subsequent narratives can pick specific translations to develop in detail (the Heyting-Bilattice translation is partially worked in [office-formalism-equivalence](../theories/predicated-governance/theorems/office-formalism-equivalence.md); the Free-algebra-Bilattice translation is sketched here but not formalized; the Heyting-Free translation is left open). The charts are not in competition; they are the result of importing three mature traditions into one work vocabulary, and the synthesis work is to make their translations precise.
