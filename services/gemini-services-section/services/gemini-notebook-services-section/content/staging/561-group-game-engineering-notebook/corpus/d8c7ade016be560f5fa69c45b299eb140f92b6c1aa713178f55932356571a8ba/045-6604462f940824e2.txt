# What do distinct $H^1$-classes look like operationally?

The [function-as-aperiodic-settling theorem](../theorems/function-as-aperiodic-settling.md), via the math's [aperiodic-regime classification](../../../../../../../../../../library/theories/humanities/theories/math/theories/categories/theories/predication-categories/theorems/aperiodic-regime-classification.md), claims that two aperiodic-regime functions are MLD-equivalent iff their classifying invariants in $H^1(\mathbf{T}^*_{\leq \omega_1}, \mathcal{F}_U^{\mathrm{univ}})$ agree. This makes "what kind of unfinishable work this is" precise — the kind is the $H^1$-class.

Open: what would two functions in *different* $H^1$-classes look like to a working community?

The math's classifying invariant is built from the firing pattern at non-Scott-continuous limit ordinals — which limit ordinals does the closure first add genuinely-new content at, and which cells does it add. Two settlings with the same firing-ordinal pattern (cell-bijection-up-to-MLD) carry the same $H^1$-class. The question is: in operational terms, what does this firing-pattern correspond to?

Constructing the correspondence requires working out [function-closure-rules](function-closure-rules.md) for distinct functions and computing their $H^1$-classes side by side; the question is downstream of that work. Without specific functions worked out, the operational reading of the firing-ordinal pattern is open.

The question matters because the cohomological-classification claim is the strongest single structural commitment of the predicated-work program; without operational meaning, the claim is purely formal.
