# Wilder Conceptions 01

**What this document is.** The owner asked for a second Fable working in parallel to push past
what one voice finds alone. This is that pass: eight conceptions, each one taking a real house
capability to a conclusion nobody in this session's documents has stated yet. None of this is
decided. All of it is meant to be argued with, not adopted whole. I read the vision note, the
suite-and-studio note, the house spec standard, the fleet audit, the cartridge/energy-bank spec,
Equivo and the self-education platform design, the tileslots and weiqi market teardowns, the
weiqi platform plan, the SHRIMP ecology brief v2, the theory-study and Equivo evidence dossiers,
and a sample of the semiotic-smart-material corpus, before writing any of this — citations below
point at what I actually read, not what I'm assuming is there. Item 1 is meant to be
uncomfortable; I mean it as a real challenge to a claim already made this session, not a
provocation for its own sake.

## 1. The earn-only claim is a projection claim, and the house's own math already says so

**The conception.** `the-suite-and-the-studio.md` says the shelf can truthfully claim "we pay
you; you are never the product being sold" — and names this as the one sentence no incumbent F2P
catalog can adopt without abandoning its business model. The house's own chemistry corpus
already contains the exact theorem that says this sentence, as stated, is not quite honest: "the
enzyme is not consumed" is never free, it is *regenerated*, and regeneration is a T-invariant
that closes on a projection while spending something real off it — "every 'not consumed' claim
is relative to a projection, and names a complement it does not mention. The complement is where
the cost lands." Earn-only economics is exactly this shape: player payout is a real cycle that
closes on the ledger's own coordinates (income → liabilities:energy → expenses:payout, balanced,
auditable), and it is genuinely not extracting money from the player. But it is not therefore
free — something off that projection is what makes the cycle worth running at all, and the
trust-copy sentence should name it rather than imply there's no complement.

**Why only this house could even ask this question.** No other studio has a chemistry corpus
proving a general theorem about when "not consumed" claims hide a complement, sitting one
services-section away from its own ledger spec. `/home/emsenn/info/lib/.../semiotic-smart-material-services-section/content/documents/articles/notes/2026/07/25/2026-07-25-note-on-fixed-points-in-chemical-reaction-networks.md`
§4.1 states the theorem generally (P-invariants are free and structural; T-invariants are paid
and the payment is off-projection) and §6 turns it into a three-way typing: freely reversible,
priced, or terminal, decidable from the network's own structure rather than argued case by case.
The energy-bank spec's `verifyPayoutInvariant` (`cartridge-and-energy-bank-spec.md` §2.4) is
provably a P-invariant-shaped check — it holds structurally over the ledger's own postings. That
is real and good. It is just not the same claim as "nothing is being spent," and conflating the
two is exactly the mistake the chemistry note exists to correct.

**Smallest real step.** Name the actual complement, in one honest sentence, for each settlement
backend: attention (the session itself, the thing the semiotic-vitrimer corpus already prices as
a solvent — see item 4), word-of-mouth growth that a paid-acquisition competitor doesn't get for
free, or genuine cross-subsidy from a premium SKU that isn't payout-gated. Add a "Carrier
Audit" subsection to the house spec standard's §8 (crosscutting concepts, alongside ledger
invariants): for every settlement flow, state which invariant it actually is — P-invariant
(free, structural, cite the balance check), T-invariant (paid, name the off-projection carrier
and its rate), or terminal (a sink; say so). This is a documentation discipline, buildable today,
against zero new code.

**Staircase.** Now — it's a truth-in-advertising fix to a claim already shipped in a design note,
not a new system.

**Where it might be wrong.** The chemistry theorem is about closed reaction networks with a
fixed stoichiometry; a game economy has an open boundary (real money enters and leaves) that a
sealed CRN does not, so the analogy could be doing more rhetorical work than mathematical work
here — this may be a case where the *shape* is a useful audit tool without being literally the
same theorem, and I should be honest that I have not shown the ledger reduces to a stoichiometric
matrix, only that it rhymes with one closely enough to be worth naming.

## 2. The closure-ordinal tower is the genre wheel — genres stop being invented and start being computed

**The conception.** One of the imported research notes in the semiotic-smart-material corpus —
written for an unrelated Wuxia-flavored engineering synthesis — already proves the mapping the
fleet needs and nobody has cited into a game document yet: Caillois' four play categories (Agon,
Alea, Mimicry, Ilinx) are literally the four settling regimes the SHRIMP brief's proved math
already names (strict/finite κ<ω H¹=0, Scott-continuous κ=ω H¹=0, transfinite-bounded, aperiodic
κ≥ω₁ H¹≠0). If that mapping holds generally — and it is stated as a proved regime taxonomy in two
independent places, not invented for the Wuxia note — then a game's *genre*, in the Caillois
sense, is not a design choice layered onto a mechanic; it is the computed regime of that
mechanic's own settling. A capture race in weiqi is Agon because it provably terminates finite
and H¹=0. A slot pull is Alea because its value enters via a random oracle at Scott-continuity. A
triple-ko fight, or Crucible's hardest daily tangle, or a SHRIMP colony's void-cone region, are
all Ilinx for the identical structural reason — H¹≠0, unsettleable from within — not because they
happen to feel vertiginous.

**Why only this house could do it.** `@561-group/math`'s obstruction engine already computes H¹
rank and regime for real inputs (`equivo-design.md` §5.3's worked example literally prints
`"regime": "transfinite"` and an H¹ rank from real code, not a genre label chosen by a designer).
The SHRIMP brief (`shrimp-ecology-brief-v2.md` §1, "Settling regimes (proved)") and the Ludic
Architecture note
(`.../semiotic-smart-material-services-section/.../2026/07/28/...ludic-architecture-and-the-semiotic-substrate...md`
§4, "The Four Regimes of Play: Cohomological Obstructions in the Tower") independently land on
the same four-way split from the same closure-ordinal machinery. No other studio has an
obstruction engine that outputs a genre classification as a side effect of computing a puzzle's
price.

**Smallest real step.** Add one field to every `CartridgeManifest` (or its future extension):
`regime: "agon" | "alea" | "mimicry" | "ilinx"`, computed — not authored — by running the game's
own settling/obstruction check where one exists (Crucible, weiqi's ko logic, Equivo's `observe`
exercises already do this; onestone's cascade could be checked against the same regime taxonomy
it currently disclaims any theory link to, per its own honest "flavor-naming ONLY" note). Where a
game has no real settling computation, the field is honestly absent, per the house standard's own
"omit, don't stub" discipline.

**Staircase.** Next — the math exists and runs in at least two packages; the missing piece is one
manifest field and the discipline of computing it rather than picking a marketing genre tag.

**Honest reason this might be wrong.** The Ludic Architecture note is explicitly a NotebookLM
Studio import — "provider-generated source-grounded analysis; it carries no execution,
governance, publication, or truth authority," per its own frontmatter — so its Caillois mapping,
however sharp, is not itself house-authored theory; it's a second, independent hit on the same
mapping from a synthesis tool, not a proof from the house's own hand. Treat it as strong
corroboration of the SHRIMP brief's proved regime taxonomy, not as a second citation of equal
weight.

## 3. Cast temperament as a conserved quantity, not an authored trait

**The conception.** The suite-and-studio note proposes named recurring cast members with stable
personas, playing as peers via intent logs. The wild version: don't author a cast member's
temperament as prose — compute it. `hol_A(γ) := A(f_n)…A(f_1) ∈ K₄`, the gauge holonomy of a
loop around the predicate category, is a proved, exactly-computable invariant of any closed loop
in a graph of relations (`gauge-connection.md`/`flat-connections-classified-by-h1.md`, cited in
`shrimp-ecology-brief-v2.md` §1: "flat connections mod gauge equivalence are classified by
H¹(N𝒞, K₄)"). A cast member's play history across the shelf — every game they've played, every
rematch, every disagreement that got settled and every one that didn't — is a graph with real
loops in it. Compute the holonomy around those loops and you get a real, non-authored,
non-erasable K₄ element per cast member: trivial holonomy means "this character never holds a
grudge, structurally"; a nontrivial twist means there's a genuine, mathematically real
unresolved tension in their play history that no amount of flavor text papered over — it's a
topological fact about the loops they've actually walked, the same way the weiqi corpus's
eternal-life positions are topological facts about the board, not writer's choices.

**Why only this house could do it.** No other studio's NPCs have an intent-log history running
through a shared cartridge/energy-bank substrate at all, let alone one sitting on top of a proved
gauge-theoretic structure that turns "personality" into a computable class. The K₄ group is
already defined once (bilattice negation/conflation automorphisms) and reused as-is at the
holonomy layer per `shrimp-ecology-brief-v2.md` §1 — this is not a metaphor grafted onto the
cast, it's the same object the SHRIMP repair mechanic already uses for "which twist a repair must
flatten or route around."

**Smallest real step.** Nothing computable exists yet because no cast, no cross-game intent-log
history, and no predicate-category realization of "games two cast members have both played"
exists. The real first step is much smaller and non-mathematical: when the cast ships (suite note
§5, directive 3), log which games each member has played against which other members, as data,
now — the graph the holonomy computation would eventually run over. Computing K₄ elements over it
is a later, harder step that should not block the cast shipping with authored personas first.

**Staircase.** Later — genuinely blocked on the cast existing, on cross-game intent-log history
accumulating, and on someone doing the real work of realizing "loop in a commitment graph between
two cast members" as an actual predicate-category object, which nothing in the corpus has done
yet for a *cast*, only for drone swarms and disclinations.

**Honest reason this might be wrong.** This may be a case of borrowing a name because it sounds
right rather than because the structure actually transfers — the theory-study dossier's own §2.2
flags exactly this failure mode ("Corpus 1... Corpus 2... read as two independent developments...
not a single document establishing the derivation direction") for adjacent claims, and I have not
shown that a cast member's play graph is actually a predicate category in the technical sense
K₄-holonomy requires, only that the shape rhymes. If it turns out not to compose, the honest
fallback is: authored personas, informed by real play-history statistics, and no claim of a
conserved invariant.

## 4. The corpus is the board: coarsening as a real game object, not a metaphor for content rot

**The conception.** `self-education-platform-design.md` already computes formal concepts (Halls)
over the weiqi glossary by real FCA, and `lantern-service/src/stability.mts` already runs a real
pebble-game rigidity check to decide whether a Hall is "stable" (its own isostatic-adjacent
language). The semiotic-smart-material corpus's imported "Physics of Forgetting" report states,
independently, the general physics this is one instance of: a vocabulary is a foam, terms with
fewer than six neighbors shrink and get absorbed (von Neumann's Law, `dA/dt = k(n-6)`), survival
is a function of connectivity, not quality, and the fix is Pickering-style "ossification" —
pinning walls so they stop moving. The wild move: stop treating this as an essay about knowledge
decay and build a real game on top of the FCA/rigidity machinery that already exists. A
"corpus-gardening" theme, riding the self-education platform's own chassis (§9's placement plan
names exactly this extension point): players don't just review cards, they *tend the concept
graph itself* — adding a citation is adding a neighbor to a foam cell; earning enough mastery to
gate a room is Pickering-pinning that cell so it stops being coarsening-eligible; a hall that
drops below isostatic (too few real connections, `stability.mts`'s own rigidity check reading
"floppy") visibly starts to fade in the UI, exactly as Lantern's light-cone already fades unreviewed
territory — the mechanism is already built, just not yet pointed at the corpus's own survival.

**Why only this house could do it.** The FCA and pebble-game code are real, tested, already
computing exactly the "is this cluster of terms connected enough to be structurally sound"
question the Physics of Forgetting note describes in general terms — `self-education-platform-design.md`
§2 and §5 ("structural mastery... a hall is stable or it isn't... this is a graph property of the
hall"). No other Duolingo-shaped product has its own curriculum's health computed by the identical
rigidity math that also validates its match-3 puzzle solvability (onestone's pebble game,
vendored into lantern per house doctrine, §8 point 5 of the platform doc). One piece of math,
three unrelated-looking uses, already true today.

**Smallest real step.** Surface `stability.mts`'s existing pebble-game verdict as player-visible
feedback in Lantern's map, today, with copy that's honest about what it is: "this cluster of
terms has [n] real connections; [below/at/above] the stable threshold." No new math — just
exposing a number the code already computes but currently only uses internally for the light-cone
dimming multiplier (platform doc §5).

**Staircase.** Now for the exposure step above; next for a full "gardening" theme that lets
players deliberately add citations/doors as a scored action rather than a passive corpus-authoring
side effect — that requires new UI and a design decision about whether players should be able to
edit the corpus at all, which is genuinely unresolved (the platform doc's own open question 3
about vendor-per-theme doesn't even reach this, since it assumes players never write to the
corpus).

**Honest reason this might be wrong.** Letting players' in-game actions influence a *shared,
real* concept graph blurs "gameplay" and "corpus editing" in a way the house has never done
before and that the corpus-normalization discipline elsewhere in this repo treats as a serious,
governed act, not a casual game action — this conception may be solving a UI problem
(make an existing number visible) by proposing a much bigger governance problem (let players write
to the graph) that doesn't actually need solving to get the good part.

## 5. Idle games with numbers that are actually true: prestige as the Perron–Frobenius eigenvector

**The conception.** The evidence dossier flags a whole cluster of idle-game time-economy theory —
"prestige-as-renormalization," a TypeScript engine sketch — as **dormant**, filed alongside the
house's real ludics curriculum but never integrated with it, and explicitly at risk of being
silently reinvented (`equivo-evidence-dossier.md` §1h: "Lantern-style progress bars/SRS could
accidentally reinvent this register without crediting it"). Separately, the chemistry corpus's
fixed-point note proves that "growth" in a reaction network is a Perron–Frobenius eigenvector
problem: an autocatalytic system has no fixed point in raw magnitude, but a real, computable fixed
point in *composition* under the projectivized map `s ↦ As/‖As‖`, with the dominant eigenvalue λ
as the actual growth rate and the eigenvector as what's conserved. An idle game's prestige
mechanic is structurally identical: a player builds a production graph (this building feeds that
one), resets, and keeps a multiplier. Today that multiplier is authored (a designer picks curve
shapes). The wild version: let the player's actual production graph be a real matrix, run
`@561-group/math` (or a small port of the same linear algebra) to find its dominant eigenvalue and
eigenvector, and price the prestige multiplier as literally λ, with the "prestige loadout" being
literally which mixture (the eigenvector) the player chose to hold fixed. Two players with the
same total output but different production topology get genuinely different, honestly computed
multipliers — the "kind incremental, honest numbers" instinct (vision note §3) taken as far as it
can go: not just no dark patterns, but no *invented* numbers at all.

**Why only this house could do it.** The math is already proven and already running
(`@561-group/math/obstruction` and its neighboring linear-algebra packages compute real spectral
objects for other games today, per `equivo-design.md`'s worked examples). No other idle-game
studio has a zero-dependency, tested spectral math package sitting one repo away and a design
culture that already treats "the math is load-bearing, honestly labeled" as a house standard
section (§13 of `house-specification-standard.md`).

**Smallest real step.** A tiny solo prototype, off to the side of any real fleet lane: a
production graph of 3-5 nodes, a matrix, `@561-group/math`'s existing eigen-tooling (or a small
new pure module if none of the 13 packages exposes it directly — this needs a real check against
the package list before claiming it's zero-effort), and one printed worked example showing a real
λ for a real hand-built graph, in the same "worked example, honest surprise kept" discipline
Equivo's §5 already models. That's a research spike, not a shipped feature.

**Staircase.** Next for the spike; later for a shipped idle game built on it, since it also needs
the idle/gambling series slot the vision note already names as "next" tier work in general.

**Honest reason this might be wrong.** Perron–Frobenius requires a nonnegative, irreducible
matrix — a real production graph with feedback loops, dead-end buildings, or negative-cost
synergy mechanics (which idle games love, for flavor and tension) may simply not satisfy the
theorem's hypotheses, and forcing every production graph in the game to stay irreducible-and-
nonnegative just to keep the math honest could make for a *worse* idle game than one with
authored curves — the theorem is real, but "real math" and "good game feel" are not guaranteed to
coincide here, and this is worth prototyping specifically to find out where they diverge.

## 6. The federation is the map: don't design the world's geometry, measure it

**The conception.** The vision note's gamified-Fediverse-client idea (§5a) and the SHRIMP MUD are
already named as "the same destination approached from three sides." The semioverse recon
confirms the one real live piece is a genuine, standards-conformant ActivityPub server with no
deploy target yet. The SHRIMP brief separately proves the Commons' event/visibility model should
be Lorentzian: a player's light cone is literally the apex cone of their causal history, and
spacelike-separated players are provably non-interfering — "the abyss between you is real
mathematics," in the Ludic Architecture note's phrasing of the identical idea. The wild
combination: once the ActivityPub server is actually deployed and federating (the semioverse
note's own "smallest honest first step"), don't hand-author the Commons' regional geography at
all. Measure the real Fediverse's actual federation delay, follower-graph distance, and delivery
latency between your instance and every other reachable instance, and let *that* be the causal
structure — literal round-trip signed-delivery time between two federated ActivityPub servers
becomes the literal spacelike/timelike separation of two Commons regions. A remote Mastodon
instance with slow, unreliable delivery to you is, honestly and un-metaphorically, "far away" in
the game's own causal-set geometry, because the same event structure the SHRIMP brief already
specifies (a Poisson-sprinkled causal set with apex/void cones) is exactly what real federation
latency measurements would populate if you fed them in as the sprinkling.

**Why only this house could do it.** This requires, simultaneously: a real ActivityPub server
capable of measuring real signed-delivery round trips (39/39 tests passing today, per
`semioverse-state-and-fediverse-client-feasibility.md`), and a proved causal-set/Lorentzian event
model already specified for exactly this game (`shrimp-ecology-brief-v2.md` §1, "Light cones are
Lorentzian (proved)"). Nobody else building an ActivityPub-based game has a causal-set theory this
literally worked out to hand federation-latency data into, and no causal-set game theorist has a
real federation server to measure against.

**Smallest real step.** Exactly the semioverse note's own top recommendation — port the legacy
SQLite KV/queue adapter, stand up one real local devbox instance, verify a live WebFinger → actor
→ inbox → outbox round trip against one real external server. This conception adds nothing to
that step; it's the thing that makes the *next* step (once a live instance exists) worth doing:
log delivery latency as a first-class metric from day one of that devbox instance, even before any
game logic exists, so the causal-set data is being collected the moment federation starts working.

**Staircase.** Later, hard-blocked on the deploy step landing — but the "log latency now, as a
metric, before there's a game" sub-step is nearly free and could ride the very first live devbox
instance with essentially no added scope.

**Honest reason this might be wrong.** Real network latency is noisy, adversarial (an instance
could throttle you to grief your game's geography), and non-stationary in a way a research
causal-set sprinkling isn't — using it as literal game geometry risks a world map that shifts
underneath players for reasons that have nothing to do with play (an admin's server having a bad
day), which is a genuinely different failure mode than a hand-authored map ever has. This might be
a beautiful idea that produces a worse, more confusing game than just designing the map.

## 7. Terminal Bets: a gambling shelf whose entire pitch is telling you which spends are permanent

**The conception.** The vision note wants a whole gambling series specifically to teach kind-
economy principles in another context. The chemistry corpus's three-way carrier typing (§6 of the
fixed-points note: freely reversible / priced / terminal, decidable structurally from a
network's own T-invariants) is a genuinely new mechanic nobody in the genre has, because nobody
in the genre wants to admit the distinction exists — the whole predatory pattern the tileslots
teardown documents (hidden odds, duplicate-as-waste, disappearing-collection panic pressure) works
*because* players can't tell which spends are recoverable and which aren't. Build the inverse: a
game (or a whole shelf register) where every wagering-adjacent mechanic is typed on-screen, using
real language borrowed honestly from the chemistry note, not invented flavor — "this spend is
freely reversible" (a real T-invariant with zero net cost, shown), "this spend is priced" (a real
T-invariant that costs a named, quantified amount off a named other resource, shown as a number,
not hidden), or "this spend is terminal" (no T-invariant exists; the game says so, plainly, before
you commit) — turning the CRN theorem's own honesty discipline into the shelf's actual UI
language, not just its backend audit tool (item 1's Carrier Audit, applied here as the player-
facing surface rather than an internal document).

**Why only this house could do it.** The gambling-services-section already has a tested Kelly-
criterion/odds/staking library (vision note §3) to compute real, honest odds; the earn-only ledger
discipline (energy-bank spec §2.4) already forbids wagering-multiplied payouts by construction.
Layering the chemistry corpus's carrier typing on top costs nothing structurally new — it's a
disclosure convention riding math and a ledger that already exist, the same move the house
standard's §13 already makes for the theory binding of other games.

**Smallest real step.** Take one already-planned gambling mechanic (even a coin-flip toy) and
write its chart of accounts in the three-way typing explicitly, by hand, as a design-doc exercise
— no code yet, just proving the typing is expressible for a real mechanic before building UI
around it.

**Staircase.** Next — it rides the "idle + gambling series" tier the vision note already stages
as next, adding a disclosure discipline rather than new scope.

**Honest reason this might be wrong.** "Terminal" is a genuinely scary word to put in front of a
player about to spend real energy, and there's a real risk that maximal honesty here reads as
maximal friction — the tileslots teardown's own finding is that near-miss psychology and pinch
points *work* on purpose; a shelf that tells players "this is permanent" before every terminal
spend might simply convert fewer players than one that doesn't, and the house needs to be honest
that "more ethical" and "more commercially viable" are not guaranteed to be the same curve here,
even though they're the right curve to be on.

## 8. The isostatic org chart: rigidity-check the fleet's own vendor graph

**The conception.** `the-suite-and-the-studio.md` already says the corporation's org chart is a
test suite — QA is the adversarial-verify pass, anti-cheat is replay determinism. Take that
literally one step further using math that already exists, twice, in this fleet, for an unrelated
purpose: `stability.mts`'s pebble-game rigidity check (vendored from onestone into lantern,
per-house-doctrine, and cited again in the self-education platform doc §5 as the hall-stability
test) is a real, working implementation of the Phillips–Thorpe isostatic-point discipline the
Physics of Forgetting note names generally: mean coordination ⟨r⟩ = 2.4 is the balance point
between floppy (too few real constraints — redundant, wasted reinvention) and over-constrained
(too many — brittle coupling that cracks under change). The fleet audit
(`fleet-spec-coverage-and-roadmap.md` gap #4) already found a real floppy instance by hand:
`onestone-service/src/accounts.mts`, ported from flickering-delve, never wired into anything — a
term with too few neighbors, exactly the kind of thing von Neumann's law predicts gets silently
absorbed or, worse, silently rots while looking maintained. And the fleet audit's own three false
"Semioverse" leads (`semioverse-state-and-fediverse-client-feasibility.md`) are the
over-constrained failure mode's mirror: three directories sharing a name with no real edges
between them, competing for the same semantic territory. The wild move: build the fleet's own
service-dependency/vendor graph (who vendors what from whom, per the house standard's §2 "vendor a
copy, documented as a port" rule) as literal graph data, and run the same pebble-game check
against it that already validates onestone's puzzles and lantern's halls — a real, computed,
CI-checkable "is the fleet itself isostatic" number, using code that already exists for a
different game.

**Why only this house could do it.** The rigidity math is real, tested, and already vendored
three separate times for three separate purposes (onestone's puzzle validation, lantern's hall
stability, and this document's own proposal to reuse it a fourth time) — a fleet with this much
self-similar math lying around, and a house-standard discipline of *naming every vendor* as a
documented port rather than a silent import, is the one place this reflexive move is actually
cheap: the vendor graph the check would run over is already required to be documented, per the
house standard's own vendor-not-import rule.

**Smallest real step.** By hand, once: list every documented vendor relationship in the fleet
(the house standard names three already — onestone→lantern's `stability.mts`, flickering-delve→
onestone's `ledger.mts`, and the "Semioverse" name collision as a non-edge) as a small graph, and
just look at its mean coordination number without writing any new code — see whether the number
is even meaningful at this scale before building tooling around it.

**Staircase.** Later — genuinely fun and probably cheap once the fleet has enough vendor
relationships to make a graph statistic meaningful, but at three or four documented edges today
it's not yet a graph with a real answer, only a promising toy.

**Honest reason this might be wrong.** The Phillips–Thorpe threshold is derived for physical
covalent-bond networks where "constraint" has a precise, uniform physical meaning (a bond angle,
a bond length); a software vendor-graph's "constraint" is not remotely uniform — vendoring five
lines of a pure function is not the same kind of edge as vendoring a whole ledger subsystem — and
treating them as equal-weight edges to compute one clean number risks exactly the "cardinality
match is not evidence" failure mode the honeycomb/drone-swarm note catches itself making
elsewhere in this corpus. This is the item I'm least sure survives contact with real data; it may
turn out to be a fun number with no real predictive content, which would still be worth knowing.
